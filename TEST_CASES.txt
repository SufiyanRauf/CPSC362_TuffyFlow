What is not tested, and why
---------------------------

Worth saying plainly, because "194 cases, all passing" invites the reader to assume more
than it should. There are no automated tests for the React screens, the sign in flow or
the map. The logic those screens depend on was deliberately moved out of them, into
src/lib and api/, so that it could be tested without a browser harness, and that is what
the cases below cover.

That move is not finished, and the honest version of the sentence is "we tested the
algorithms and the security model" rather than "there is no logic left in the screens".
Three pieces of it were still inside ProfileView.tsx, the largest file here and the one
with no tests of its own, and the two that matter most had to agree with
db/firestore.rules while the only thing holding them in step was a comment saying so.
They are now in src/lib/profileEdits.ts and are the Profile screen rules suite below:
the interest slug, the two tag ceilings, and the class time validation. Two of those
cases read the rules file itself rather than repeating its numbers, so the client and
the database drift apart in a test run instead of in front of a student.

Leaving for good arrived later and is the one irreversible thing the app does, so it is
worth saying exactly how far the cases below go. Tested: the database allows each step
and refuses it on somebody else, a class meeting left behind with no profile above it is
still its owner to delete, and every sentence the sign in code can say is either allowed
through to a panel or listed as belonging to the login card. Not tested: the order the
three steps run in, and the guard that stops Save changes writing the profile back while
the delete is in flight. Those two are checked by reading the source rather than by
calling anything, which is weaker, and it is said here rather than left to look like
coverage.

Still checked by hand only: the session storage draft recovery in that same file, the
Delete account panel and the reset card as screens, and every other screen as a screen.
The last of those is what a component harness would cover, and there is no component
harness in this project.

Two consequences are worth naming rather than leaving for a reader to find. FR-1 and
FR-2 are proved at the database rather than at the screen: the evidence is that the
rules accept a good write and refuse a bad one, not that the form submits. And the demo
mode, which lets somebody try the app with no account, reads bundled rows instead of
Firestore, so no security rules case can reach it; it is checked by hand too.
